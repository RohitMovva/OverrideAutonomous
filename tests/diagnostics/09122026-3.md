# Curved-path diagnosis: 09122026-3

The dominant observed errors are an outside-wheel speed deficit in the bend,
followed by excess rotation on exit and lateral error that remains when the
trajectory ends. There is no single demonstrated gain or track-width correction
that fixes all three. Position here means the robot's odometry estimate; the log
contains no independent measurement of its physical position.

## Reference verified

`/mnt/sdcard/routes/turn_test.txt` contains 224 trajectory samples and two actions.
Every exported pose matches the logged Goal within 0.0000005. Export SHA256:
`d696723e0b55c5a9d3f9ab7a1b95a8070df00420f4041cc668dbc06f992c521d`.
The editable source is `VexAutonomousPlanner/src/routes/turn_test.txt`.
The exported reference integrates to 42.858767 inches and -89.999756 degrees;
its sampled geometric length is 42.854865 inches. Coordinate reflection,
angular-velocity sign, and feet-to-inches conversion are consistent for this run.

There is a real, smaller interpolation bug in `generate_motion_profile`: the
sample interval duration assumes constant acceleration, but both distance and
velocity were independently interpolated linearly in time. Distance must be
quadratic in time. For example, the first 10 ms interval advances the reference
0.017024 inches while its velocity integral is only 0.005690 inches. The worst
single-interval discrepancy is 0.011334 inches. This is too small to explain the
1.17-inch final error, but gives RAMSETE mutually inconsistent pose and velocity
references. `sample_profile_state` now integrates velocity exactly inside each
profile interval. Regenerate the route to apply this fix; the existing SD export
has deliberately not been overwritten.

The generator also stops sampling before the exact end: the last sample is at
2.23 s, v=0.927729 in/s, y=-0.051099 versus the node's y=-0.041. The missing
0.0101-inch tail is minor here. Appending an arbitrary-time final sample would
require changing the follower too: it consumes one row per 10 ms and ignores
row timestamps. Endpoint scheduling/settling is not changed in this patch.

All times below are relative to the first control sample at 2.254 s in the log.

* Entering/taking the bend (about 0.9–1.3 s), the left outside wheel is commanded
  roughly 34 in/s but delivers roughly 29 in/s. The right wheel is near zero.
  Twenty-one left commands hit 127; twenty-three are at least 126, between
  0.93 and 1.19 s, with mean speed error 5.68 in/s on those near-saturated ticks.
  This reduces turning response and causes lag in heading and position.
  A speed setpoint below the 44.956 in/s cap is not necessarily attainable under
  turning load. Raising PID gains cannot create additional motor voltage.
* On exit the heading error changes sign. At 1.7 s it is already about +4.06
  degrees (goal minus actual). By 2.0 s, the robot is at x=-22.752 versus
  goal x=-23.700, while y is essentially on target. The terminal error is
  primarily lateral, not simply stopping short.
* RAMSETE's gain `2*zeta*sqrt(w_ref^2+b*v_ref^2)` and its lateral correction
  `b*v_ref*sinc(e_theta)*e_y` both shrink as the reference stops. The follower
  unconditionally stops at the end of the dataset, with no pose-settling phase.
  Existing lateral error therefore persists. Increasing B alone is not an
  endpoint controller and would also change transient demands in the bend.

## Wheel/IMU disagreement: do not calibrate from one scalar

The logged controller wheel differential integrates to -21.397 inches. Filtered
measured wheel differential is only -18.955 inches (88.6% delivered), yet the IMU
reports MORE rotation than the -90.81 degrees implied by those commanded wheels
at the configured 13.5-inch width. Thus wheel-speed shortfall alone cannot explain
the final yaw. These are approximate discrete integrals; the measured wheel
velocity is observer output, not an independent ground-speed measurement.

The existing whole-run effective-width fit gives 12.08 inches, R²=0.958. But
ratios from raw cumulative encoder travel and IMU rotation over successive
windows vary sharply: 15.66 inches at 0.6–0.9 s, 10.74 at 0.9–1.3 s, and 8.71
at 1.3–1.7 s. Transient slip, sensor latency/error, and the drive model can all
contribute. A global fit is not sufficient evidence to change 13.5 to 12.08.

The right motor encoder travel is 1312 versus 1638 ticks: a 19.9% disagreement.
The left pair records 2512 versus 2513 ticks. Current source selects right motor
index 1 (port 15), consistent with the logged cumulative right distance; its
comment still names the other motor's old port 9, while this boot logs port 12.
Selecting one encoder avoids averaging the disagreement but does not establish
which reading matches ground travel. Check the right pair's encoder, gear ratio,
and mechanical coupling. Motor-health samples show no thermal fault or detected
loaded cutout during this short run; they cannot exclude other hardware faults.

## Changes and next measurement

Implemented:

* Correct position interpolation in the planner, with acceleration, braking,
  cruise, nonuniform-interval, and boundary regression checks.
* Remove the undefined third-motor encoder read in odometry logging. Log the
  same sensor snapshot used for the odometry calculation.
* Enable the already-supported MP and RAMSETE output streams for future logs.
* Add `tests/analyze_curve.py` to reproduce the reference match, encoder
  disagreement, saturation, and rotation-budget measurements.

Next physical checks, in order:

1. Resolve or independently characterize the right encoder disagreement. Measure
   actual heading and ground displacement so IMU/drive slip can be separated.
2. Regenerate this same curve and make a deliberately slower comparison run
   (for example a 25% lower route velocity limit). Check whether outside-wheel
   saturation and the heading sign reversal diminish. This is a diagnostic
   comparison, not a calibrated permanent velocity limit.
3. Once wheel measurements are trustworthy, use the new reference/RAMSETE logs
   to distinguish requested yaw, delivered wheel differential, and IMU yaw.
   Fit feedforward/turning limits from multiple speeds and both turn directions.
   If wheel tracking remains good but yaw does not, investigate a gyro-rate
   feedback loop rather than compensating everything with wheel PID gains.
4. Add an explicit, bounded endpoint pose controller if the task requires tighter
   stopped accuracy. A zero-velocity RAMSETE reference alone cannot remove
   lateral displacement on a differential drive.
